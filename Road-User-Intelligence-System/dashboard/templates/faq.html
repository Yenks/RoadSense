Documentation & System FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about CivicShield's video analytics pipeline, legal baselines, and safety monitoring capabilities.

gavel 1. What legal regulations form the baseline for violation detection?

CivicShield enforces the legal framework established in the Ghana Road Traffic Regulations 2012 (L.I. 2180), specifically the statutory 30.0 km/h school zone speed limit. Pedestrian right-of-way logic is governed by Ghana Road Traffic Act 2004 (Act 683), requiring drivers to stop and give precedence to pedestrians on or entering a marked zebra crossing.

videocam 2. How does CivicShield detect vehicles and pedestrians from drone video?

The pipeline utilizes a fine-tuned YOLOv8 medium object detection neural network trained specifically on aerial/drone imagery (VisDrone dataset). Detected bounding boxes are passed to ByteTrack to assign persistent identity tracking numbers across consecutive video frames.

speed 3. How is vehicle speed calculated accurately in km/h from 2D pixels?

Pixel movement is mapped to physical meters using a planar homography camera calibration built from 8 surveyed ground reference coordinates at the Tamale school zone site. Kalman filtering and optical flow checking smooth trajectory jitter, producing reliable speed readings in real-world km/h.

warning 4. What constitutes a "Failed to Yield" safety incident?

A failure to yield incident is triggered when a vehicle maintains a speed higher than 8.0 km/h while entering the defined 30-meter approach zone while a pedestrian is active within or within 2.0 meters of the crossing boundary.

lock 5. Who can access the processed video results and evidence logs?

The public overview page provides high-level project information. Full analysis reports, vehicle trajectory speeds, and image snapshot evidence are strictly access-controlled via Supabase Authentication, requiring an authorized account.
